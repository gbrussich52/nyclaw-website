# NYClaw: clearer reasons to talk, with a wider offer

The homepage, About page, services and NYC/Westchester location pages previously led with custom-agent terminology, OODA/ROI language and repeated “not a template” comparisons. Some service pages claimed savings or output increases without supporting client evidence. Consulting buttons said “Book” while opening an inquiry form.

The site now starts with familiar work: an inquiry awaiting a reply, an estimate to prepare and details copied between tools. Approved client examples appear earlier and retain their exact approved wording. Separate paths cover AI assistants, workflow builds, marketing/follow-up and advice/roadmaps. The law-firm Workflow Blueprint remains a specialist assessment within that broader agency, with its fictional example labeled.

Booking buttons open the existing free30-minute Calendly event. Inquiry labels ask where work gets stuck; the form explains that sending it does not book a call or start paid work. Existing field values, API handling, prices, roadmap credits, privacy controls and assessment backend remain unchanged. Unsupported savings, output and response-time claims are removed from the edited core pages.

The research supports clearer expectations and problem-first language, not a predicted conversion lift. Primary sources and limitations: [research notes](../research/2026-10-07-positioning.md).

Lesson: a strong claim needs proof and a button needs to deliver what it says. The reusable scripts/check-positioning-copy.mjs guard protects approved cases, catches repeated unsupported claims and checks booking/offer placement. It adds no schedule or dependency. Conversion must be measured from actual visits, inquiries and qualified calls; a calendar click alone is not a booked call.

Validation:44 existing source tests, TypeScript and production build pass; editorial guard passes; independent claim/CTA review completed. Desktop/mobile checks cover nine public routes and fictional intercepted contact responses. Hosted release proof is recorded with the pull request. No real test inquiry or email was sent.
