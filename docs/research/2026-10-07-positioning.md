# Positioning NYClaw around work that gets stuck

Checked 7 October 2026. NYClaw should speak to owners and operations leads at small service businesses and professional firms through recognizable work: a new inquiry awaiting a reply, an estimate that needs approval, a customer update with no owner, or completed work waiting to be billed. These are **editorial hypotheses to test with buyers**, not quotations from interviews or evidence that each sector has been validated.

The [Federal Reserve Banks' 2026 employer-firm report](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey/2026-report-on-employer-firms) found that 57% reported reaching customers or growing sales as an operational challenge, 46% hiring or retaining qualified staff, and 73% increased costs as a financial challenge. Among AI users, 46% cited accuracy and 43% adapting tools to their needs; only 7% said AI was fully integrated. This supports starting with the handoff and its reviewer, then asking whether existing software can handle it. It does **not** measure demand for NYClaw or prove that automation improves sales. The 2025 survey had 6,525 employer-firm responses and used a weighted convenience sample, not a random sample.

[NFIB's 2025 small-business technology survey and methodology](https://www.nfib.com/news/press-release/new-nfib-report-how-small-businesses-incorporate-tech-and-ai-advancements/) found 57% of small employers had introduced new or significantly improved technology in the previous two years. It describes 521 respondents from an emailed sample of 20,000 NFIB members in March 2025. That membership and response pattern limit generalization. The copy should therefore ask what tools the buyer already uses before suggesting a new build.

Two narrower examples make the broad proposition tangible. [Clio's 2024 secret-shopper exercise](https://www.clio.com/about/press/clio-latest-legal-trends-report/) emailed 500 law firms; 33% responded, and 18% of responders provided clear next steps or cost information. A law-firm example can show a staff-owned intake reply, without promising more clients or implying that a document's contents or signature are verified. [Housecall Pro's 2025 survey](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-customer-service-report-trends-statistics/) of 1,040 U.S. household decision-makers found 80% consider online booking when choosing a service professional. A home-service example can show a booking confirmation or schedule update. Clio studied a particular inquiry scenario; Housecall Pro measured homeowner preferences, not contractors' budgets or actual purchase behavior. Both publishers sell software to the populations they study.

Proposed customer-facing words should invite recognition rather than assert a buyer's pain: “Where does the next handoff pause?” “Who replies when a new request comes in?” “What does your current software already do?” These are NYClaw's drafts, not surveyed customer language. The [GOV.UK interface-writing guidance](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/writing-for-user-interfaces) favors familiar, direct words and clear standalone links; [Nielsen Norman Group's link-label guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/better-link-labels/) favors labels that accurately predict the destination. Neither establishes a conversion lift for this site.

Keep the agency range visible: marketing help, advice, custom workflow implementation, and the Workflow Blueprint as **one** assessment offer. The Blueprint can recommend configuring existing tools, buying, building, or keeping a step manual. Keep approved real business-case copy exact; do not turn a fictional workflow example into a client result. No active price change follows from this research.

Each call to action should name the next screen: **Book a free 30-minute call** opens the actual calendar; **See real client work** goes to the work examples; **Tell us what's stuck** opens the contact form; **See a fictional workflow example** goes to the Blueprint example; **Request a workflow assessment** submits an inquiry and confirms receipt, not acceptance of paid work. Check destinations before publishing. Use an ordinary invitation, with no fake scarcity, assumed fear, or unsupported return-on-investment claim.

The editorial pass also follows [Orwell’s original essay](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/): use concrete meaning, active verbs and familiar words; cut stale comparisons and needless abstractions. This is a writing discipline, not evidence of a sales effect.
