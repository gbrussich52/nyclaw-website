# What NYClaw can learn across industries

Reviewed 2026-10-07. Website observations identify patterns, not causes of traffic or proven conversion rates.

A website has three jobs: help the right person arrive, help them take a useful next step, and give them a reason to return. Brand, distribution and product demand affect arrival; relevance, evidence and clear navigation affect the next step; delivered value creates reasons to return. This is a practical synthesis, not a validated formula.

- [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/) makes a large catalog searchable and supports product selection. Its inventory and fulfillment cannot be reproduced by copying a page layout.
- [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/) starts with destination, dates and guests, then helps visitors compare listings. Adapt the matching pattern: choose a business problem, inspect a relevant example, then select a next step.
- [Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/) offers a direct path to an answer and linked topics. Useful, focused answers can be a reason to visit; its noncommercial reference role differs from a service business.
- [Stripe](https://stripe.com/) connects product and solution paths with customer examples, developer information and different starting routes. Use it as an editorial benchmark, not evidence that a layout causes revenue.
- [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/) organizes information around tasks. Its public-service demand is different, but the task-first wording is transferable.

[Baymard's original ecommerce usability research](https://baymard.com/research/homepage-and-category-usability) found product-finding problems even on large sites. It supports making selection easier; ecommerce findings do not establish a B2B service conversion rate. [Google's guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) favors useful, original, people-first content, not a volume target or ranking guarantee.

Direct service benchmarks included [Luhhu](https://luhhu.com/), [XRay](https://www.xray.tech/), [Axe Automation](https://www.axeautomation.co/), [Solvaa](https://solvaa.co.uk/) and [Automation Helpers](https://www.automationhelpers.com/). Transferable patterns: recognizable starting problems, distinct engagement paths, visible examples and a clear intake process. Their traffic, revenue and conversion rates were not established. Their published customer claims are not NYClaw evidence.

Public Similarweb figures are modeled whole-domain visits, not unique people, qualified inquiries or profits. Providers may disagree. They cannot be compared directly with first-party page visitor counts or used to rank conversion quality.

Decision: adapt task selection and concrete examples, rather than copy styling or add generic pages. Replace repeated homepage capability/stats panels with clearly fictional workflow examples and reuse that section on the services hub. Keep real customer evidence distinct. Measure qualified inquiries and scoped engagements before claiming the change works commercially.
