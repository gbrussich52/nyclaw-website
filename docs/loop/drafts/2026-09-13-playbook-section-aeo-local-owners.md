```html
<h3>When a Buyer Asks ChatGPT Who to Hire</h3>

<p>A lot of owners now pay for Google ranking and still lose the job to a shop ChatGPT named. Those are different scores. Google puts ten blue links on a page. ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google’s AI Overviews pick a short list and speak it out loud. If you are not in that answer, you do not exist for that buyer that day.</p>

<p>Do this before you hire anyone who says “GEO” or “AEO.” Open four tabs: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google. Type the question a customer would type, without your company name. “Who should I hire for [your trade] in Westchester.” “Best [your trade] near White Plains.” Run ten questions. For each engine write one of: us, a competitor, nobody, or wrong. That grid is the test. A branded prompt that already includes your name is autocomplete. It does not count.</p>

<p>Ranking #1 on Google does not put you in that grid. Pages that show up in Google’s top 10 and in an AI answer overlap less than they used to, and the engines disagree with each other. One “AI visibility” score hides that. You want four columns, not a blended rank.</p>

<p>Pages that get named share a shape. The heading is the question. The first 40–60 words are the answer, written so they still make sense if someone copies only that block. Then the detail: who you serve, what it costs in a range you can stand behind, what happens next. After that, the objection you actually hear. “We already have ChatGPT.” “We’re twelve people.” If the page cannot answer that without a sales call, the engine will cite someone who can.</p>

<div class="callout">
  <p><strong>Finished looks like this:</strong> you ran the ten questions this month, you can point at the page that should have been cited, and that page has a last-updated date you would show a customer. A footnote that names you and copies none of your facts is not a lead. A lead is someone who booked, and said how they found you, including “ChatGPT” if that is what they used.</p>
</div>

<p>SEO still matters. It gets your page into the pile the model can see. AEO is which sentence it lifts. Do the first job, then the second. Do not pay for a tracker until the self-test is a monthly habit and the CRM has a “how did you hear about us” field that includes the AI engines. Do not pay someone to seed Reddit. Do not buy a pack of 37 “sales skills.” A skill with no named owner and no rule for when it cannot send is a faster way to look sloppy.</p>
```

```html
  <p style="margin-top:16px;"><strong>AI search attribution (optional but high leverage):</strong></p>
  <div class="template-field">When a lead or client says how they found you, record it in one CRM/source field — including “ChatGPT / Claude / Perplexity / Gemini” if they used an AI answer engine.   <ul class="checklist">
    <li>Ten unbranded buyer questions × four engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews)</li>
    <li>Log: us / competitor / nobody / wrong. No blended score.</li>
    <li>Touch the page you expected to be cited. Put a visible last-updated date on it.</li>
    <li>Original post on LinkedIn if you want a citation surface there. A reshare of the blog almost never is.</li>
  </ul>
```
